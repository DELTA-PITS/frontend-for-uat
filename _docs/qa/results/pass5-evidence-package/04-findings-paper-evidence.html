1. How the Paper Author Should Use This Page

This page maps paper-facing claims to the independent QA evidence. It is not a replacement paper draft. The purpose is to help the author distinguish what is empirically supported, partially supported, contradicted by independent testing, not independently evaluated, or requires reconciliation with missing/historical evidence.

Recommended tone: report the implemented provenance mechanism positively where evidence supports it, while qualifying identity attribution, publisher isolation, validation, and historical performance claims. Avoid both “the system fully works” and “the system failed” framings.

3. Suggested Evidence-Accurate Framing

“Independent technical QA confirmed that the prototype's core provenance path—document hashing, registry persistence, blockchain anchoring, and public re-verification—operates end-to-end on the tested stack. The same QA pass identified unresolved limitations in publisher identity attribution, cross-publisher record isolation, file-content validation, and selected error-handling paths.”

This wording is deliberately stronger than “there were bugs” but more accurate than “100% functional pass.” It separates the successfully demonstrated provenance core from the surrounding security and robustness limitations.

4. What PITS Demonstrates — and What It Does Not

Demonstrated

  • SHA-256 integrity comparison of uploaded bytes.
  • Registry persistence in PostgreSQL.
  • Hash anchoring/read-back on the configured Anvil chain.
  • Public verification without login.
  • Tampered and unregistered artifacts fail verification.
  • Publisher-role gate rejects a user without the role.

Not demonstrated / currently limited

  • Reliable publisher identity attribution in stored records.
  • Cross-publisher data isolation.
  • Public-chain production durability/governance.
  • Institutional authority of the publisher account.
  • Factual correctness of document content.
  • Increase in public trust or participation.

5. Performance Evidence for the Paper

The final QA pass produced a new reproducible local benchmark: three 60-second runs, 10 users, 1 user/second spawn rate, 8,213 total requests, mean throughput approximately 46.32 requests/second. The aggregate failure rate was 10.2%, entirely caused by the known missing-issuer-identity assertion. Representative Run-1 aggregate mean latency was 24.5 ms and p95 46 ms.

Do not write this as a replacement for the historical production/UAT benchmark. The environment, test-code version and TEST_MODE/auth configuration of the historical 8,880/8,245/8,165 figures cannot be proven equivalent.

6. Recommended Discussion Points

  • Positive: cryptographic provenance consistency is demonstrably functional across real local components.
  • Identity limitation: source verification requires both role gating and durable actor attribution; the second is currently missing.
  • Authorization limitation: role-based access is insufficient without tenant-level data isolation.
  • Prototype blockchain limitation: Anvil provides a verifiable transaction mechanism for the PoC but is ephemeral and must not be described as public-chain durability.
  • Validation limitation: file extension/MIME checks are not equivalent to PDF content validation.
  • Reproducibility lesson: raw artifacts and exact test-code versions materially affect the credibility of performance claims.

7. Recommended Limitations Text Themes

The paper should explicitly state that the independent QA did not constitute a formal penetration test; did not assess citizen trust outcomes, institutional adoption or factual content correctness; did not validate public-blockchain behavior; and did not complete accessibility, real-device, full DR or maximum-capacity stress testing.
